How to Calculate Your Kua Number and Use It for Better Feng Shui

A Kua number is a personal energy value between 1 and 9 that Eight Mansions feng shui assigns to each person based on birth year and gender, determining which compass directions support their luck, health, and relationships. Also known as the Ming Gua or life gua, this number splits everyone into an East Group or West Group and pairs each person with four auspicious and four inauspicious directions.

Calculating your own number takes your birth year and gender, plus the exact date if you were born in January or early February, run through a simple digit-reduction formula. Men subtract from 10 and women add 5, or 9 and 6 for anyone born in 2000 or later.

Once calculated, your Kua number reveals four lucky directions, Sheng Qi, Tien Yi, Nien Yen, and Fu Wei, tied respectively to wealth, health, relationships, and personal growth, alongside four unlucky directions to avoid for sleeping, sitting, or working.

What Is a Kua Number in Feng Shui and Why Does It Matter?

A Kua number is your personal number from 1 to 9 in Eight Mansions feng shui, and it matters because it matches you with four compass directions that support you and four to avoid. Those directions guide where you face at your desk, which way you sleep and how well your entrance suits you. The sections below cover how this number shapes personal energy and how it splits into East and West groups tied to the five elements.

How does your Kua number relate to personal energy and luck in feng shui?

A Kua number relates to personal energy and luck by mapping an individual's birth data to a specific trigram, element, and set of compass directions that either support or drain their chi. This personal number is more formally known as the Ming Gua (命卦), or life gua, and is sometimes called the life star. According to 8Mansions.com, a person's gua number can be found through a simple equation or a reference table.

Each Kua number carries four favorable and four unfavorable directions. The unfavorable ones are not neutral: the Wu Gui (Five Ghosts) direction carries Fire energy linked to gossip and backstabbing, while the Huo Hai (Mishap) direction carries Earth chi tied to hardship and obstacles. Consistently facing your best directions, rather than your worst, is the practical foundation the entire system is built on.

What are the key differences between East Group and West Group Kua numbers?

The key differences between East Group and West Group Kua numbers are the specific directions each group should favor. Kua numbers are split into two categories: East Group and West Group. People with Kua numbers 1, 3, 4, and 9 belong to the East Group and benefit from facing:

  • North
  • South
  • East
  • Southeast

People with Kua numbers 2, 6, 7, and 8 belong to the West Group and benefit from facing:

  • West
  • Northwest
  • Southwest
  • Northeast

This grouping traces back to the bagua, a set of eight trigrams built from broken and unbroken lines representing yin and yang, giving eight possible combinations. Couples with clashing groups face a real practical conflict, since East and West directions oppose each other. The couples section below covers how to share spaces between two groups.

How do Kua numbers connect to the five elements and directions in feng shui?

Kua numbers connect to the five elements through the trigram each number is assigned, since every trigram in the bagua corresponds to one of the five elements: Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, or Water. This Wuxing system describes how elements interact and appears across Chinese philosophy, including astrology, medicine, and the I Ching, not just feng shui. Each trigram also carries astronomical, geographic, and anatomical correspondences within traditional Chinese metaphysics, which is why a single Kua number influences far more than direction alone.

In practice, this means a homeowner's element governs which directions strengthen their energy and which forms or shapes complement their space. Understanding this elemental layer is what separates a superficial direction lookup from a genuinely personalized feng shui approach, and it sets up the calculation steps covered next.

How Do You Accurately Calculate Your Personal Kua Number?

Calculating a Kua number requires only your birth year and gender, applied through a simple digit-reduction formula. The steps below cover the information needed, separate male and female formulas, calendar adjustments, and common verification checks.

What information do you need (birth year, gender) to calculate your Kua number?

The information needed to calculate your Kua number is your birth year and your gender, plus the exact birth date if you were born in January or early February. Males and females use different formulas on the same birth year, and each person has a Kua number between 1 and 9. One nuance matters here: the feng shui year follows the Chinese solar calendar and starts at Li Chun, the start of spring, which falls on February 3, 4 or 5 depending on the year. Anyone born in January, or in February before that date, belongs to the previous year, so confirm the year before starting the math. Gathering these data points first prevents miscalculation later.

How do you calculate a Kua number for males step by step?

Calculating a Kua number for males follows a fixed subtraction formula applied to the birth year. The steps are:

  1. Add the last two digits of the birth year together.
  2. Reduce the sum to a single digit by adding its digits again if needed.
  3. Subtract that single digit from 10 to get the Kua number.

For example, a male born in March 1969 adds 6 + 9 = 15, then reduces 1 + 5 = 6, then subtracts 10 - 6 = 4, giving a Kua number of 4. Males born in 2000 or later subtract the single digit from 9 instead of 10, and a result of 0 counts as 9.

How do you calculate a Kua number for females step by step?

Calculating a Kua number for females follows a fixed addition formula applied to the birth year. The steps are:

  1. Add the last two digits of the birth year together.
  2. Reduce the sum to a single digit by adding its digits again if needed.
  3. Add 5 to that single digit, then reduce again if the result is two digits.

For example, a female born in March 1969 adds 6 + 9 = 15, reduces 1 + 5 = 6, adds 6 + 5 = 11, then reduces 1 + 1 = 2, giving a Kua number of 2. Females born in 2000 or later add 6 instead of 5. Note that a calculated result of 5 is never final; males adjust to 2, and females adjust to 8, since the Kua system excludes 5 entirely.

How do you correctly adjust your birth year if you were born in January or February?

You correctly adjust your birth year by checking the solar calendar instead of defaulting to the calendar year printed on your birth certificate. The feng shui year begins at Li Chun, the start of spring, which falls on February 3, 4 or 5 depending on the year, so anyone born in January, or in February before that date, belongs to the previous year for Kua purposes. A calendar of the Chinese solar terms, or the calculator above, confirms the correct year before you run any formula. Some schools count from Chinese New Year instead, which can change the result for birthdays between late January and mid-February.

Once the correct solar year is confirmed, the calculation proceeds the same way: add the last two digits together and reduce to a single digit. For instance, someone born in 1974 adds 7 + 4 = 11, then reduces 1 + 1 = 2, before applying the male or female adjustment. Skipping this check is one of the most common sources of an incorrect Kua number, since a birthday in the first days of February can shift the entire result.

How can you double-check your Kua number to avoid common calculation mistakes?

You can double-check your Kua number by rerunning the formula, verifying your gender-specific step, and confirming your solar year placement. Three checkpoints catch nearly every error:

  • Digit reduction: Confirm the last two digits of the birth year were added and reduced to a true single digit before applying the male subtraction or female addition step.
  • Gender formula: Verify males subtracted from 10 (or 9 if born in 2000 or later) and females added 5 (or 6 if born in 2000 or later), since swapping these formulas is a frequent mistake.
  • The number 5 exception: Remember that a raw result of 5 is never the final Kua number; it converts to 2 for males and 8 for females.

In practice, the solar-year boundary trips up more people than the arithmetic itself. Anyone born in January or early February should treat that check as the first verification step, not an afterthought. With your Kua number confirmed, the next step is understanding what it reveals about your compatible directions.

How Do Kua Numbers Differ for Men, Women, and Children?

Kua numbers differ for men, women, and children primarily through the calculation formula and gender-specific starting values, not through the meaning of the number itself. The following sections cover child calculations, gender-based differences, and couple compatibility.

How should you calculate a child's Kua number and at what age does it matter most?

A child's Kua number is calculated the same way as an adult's, using birth year and gender, with no separate children's formula. Parents add the last two digits of the birth year until reaching a single digit, then apply the male or female adjustment (subtracting from 10 for boys, adding 5 for girls, using 9 and 6 instead for children born in 2000 or later). The Kua number applies from birth, since it is fixed by birth year rather than by developmental stage.

In practice, most families find the Kua number becomes useful once a child has their own bedroom, since bed placement and study desk orientation rely on knowing that child's auspicious directions. Applying Kua-based direction guidance to a nursery or shared room offers limited value until the child occupies a defined personal space.

How do gender differences affect your Kua number and auspicious directions?

Gender affects your Kua number by determining which formula variable is applied to the birth year, which in turn produces a different number and a different set of auspicious directions for men and women born in the same year. A male and female born in the identical year will typically receive different Kua numbers, because the male formula subtracts from a base number while the female formula adds to one.

Each Kua number, regardless of gender, carries the same four auspicious directions: Sheng Qi for prosperity, Tien Yi for health, Nien Yen for relationships, and Fu Wei for personal growth. Sheng Qi in particular is the direction people in business should tap into to advance wealth acquisition. The directions themselves are not gendered; only the path to arriving at the correct Kua number depends on gender.

How should couples with different Kua numbers harmonize their feng shui?

Couples with different Kua numbers should harmonize their feng shui by prioritizing shared spaces around one partner's directions while reserving personal areas, such as a home office, for the other partner's favorable directions.

A practical approach separates spaces by function:

  • Main living areas, such as the living room or kitchen, are typically oriented toward the primary breadwinner's auspicious directions.
  • Bedrooms can incorporate both partners' Nien Yen relationship direction where layout allows, supporting connection despite differing Kua groups.
  • Personal spaces, such as a home office or reading nook, use the individual's own directions rather than a shared compromise.

It's worth noting that Eight Mansions practitioners themselves disagree on how rigidly to apply this formula; some Hong Kong and Taiwan-based consultants view parts of the Western interpretation as oversimplified. Because of this, treating direction conflicts as one factor among many, rather than a deal-breaker, tends to produce more livable results for couples.

How Can You Use Your Kua Number to Improve Your Home's Feng Shui?

You can use your Kua number to improve your home's feng shui by orienting daily activities toward your four auspicious directions. This applies to eating, watching TV, working, meeting, and studying, while sleeping with your head pointed toward one of these directions. The sections below cover entrance alignment, bed placement, desk orientation, and renter-friendly applications.

How do you align your main entrance with your Kua number for better energy?

Aligning your main entrance means orienting your front door, or the direction you face when standing at it, toward one of your four auspicious directions rather than an inauspicious one. Among the four favorable directions, Tien Yi, the health direction, is specifically recommended for improving physical wellbeing, making it a strong choice for households prioritizing health outcomes.

Couples often complicate entrance alignment because partners frequently have different auspicious directions. Running each partner's birth date through the calculator above shows each partner's directions so you can compare them, and the couples section above covers how to split rooms when they differ.

How should you position your bed based on your personal Kua directions?

You should position your bed so your head points toward one of your four personal auspicious directions while sleeping. This directional alignment is considered one of the two placements feng shui practitioners weigh most heavily, alongside desk positioning, because bedrooms represent where people spend extended, restorative time.

Your Ming Gua, or Personal Trigram, is based on your year of birth and reveals your best personal directions, giving you the specific compass points to test against your bed's headboard orientation. If the ideal direction is not possible due to room layout, the closest achievable auspicious direction is preferable to defaulting to an inauspicious one. Consistent nightly alignment, rather than occasional adjustment, is what practitioners point to as the difference-maker in noticeable results over time.

How can you orient your desk and workspace to support focus and success?

You can orient your desk by facing one of your Kua number's auspicious directions while seated at your workspace, whether at home or in an office. Facing a favorable direction while working, meeting, or studying is treated with the same priority as mealtime and TV-viewing orientation. If a wall, window, or fixed layout prevents facing an auspicious direction directly, angling the desk as close as possible to that direction, or repositioning the chair rather than the entire desk, can achieve a similar effect without requiring major renovation.

How can renters or people in shared housing still apply Kua number principles?

Renters and people in shared housing can still apply Kua number principles by adjusting furniture placement, bed orientation, and desk direction within existing layouts rather than making structural changes. Since renters typically cannot repaint, alter walls, or renovate, working with movable elements, beds, desks, chairs, and small furnishings, remains the most accessible way to align with personal auspicious directions.

Shared housing adds another layer of complexity when roommates or family members belong to different Kua groups. In these situations, prioritizing auspicious alignment in private, personal spaces such as one's own bedroom or desk area, while treating shared common areas more neutrally, allows each person to benefit individually without requiring agreement on a single directional scheme. Small, thoughtful entryway details, such as well-placed house numbers or plaques that clearly define the entrance, support this same principle of working within what already exists rather than against it.

How Can Your Kua Number Guide the Feng Shui of Your Home's Exterior?

Your Kua number extends beyond the interior of your home, shaping how you approach pathways, landscaping, and curb appeal. The exterior acts as the first filter for chi entering your property, so aligning it with your auspicious directions reinforces the energy you invite indoors. The following sections cover walkway placement, outdoor lighting, and overall exterior energy flow.

How do you position pathways, walkways, and steps based on auspicious directions?

Pathways, walkways, and steps should guide chi toward your home's entrance from one of your four auspicious directions rather than from an inauspicious one. A curved or gently winding path slows and distributes energy more evenly than a straight, direct line, which can send chi rushing too quickly into the home. Steps leading to the entrance benefit from solid, uncracked surfaces, since damaged walkways are believed to disrupt the smooth flow of energy toward the front door. If your existing layout channels movement from a Jue Ming, or Total Loss, direction, consider redirecting foot traffic with plantings or a curved extension rather than rebuilding the entire path.

How can landscaping and outdoor lighting support your Kua number?

Landscaping and outdoor lighting support your Kua number by reinforcing your auspicious directions with healthy plant life and consistent illumination, while avoiding harsh or blocked features along inauspicious ones. Well-placed lighting activates chi after dark, particularly along the direction associated with your Sheng Qi, or Prosperity, orientation, and overgrown or dying plants along any auspicious direction should be trimmed or replaced. Landscaping choices, such as rounded shrubs, layered greenery, or a mix of flowering and structural plants, work best when they feel balanced rather than cluttered. Outdoor lighting fixtures placed near the entrance also help define the space clearly.

How should you think about your curb appeal in relation to feng shui energy flow?

Curb appeal in relation to feng shui energy flow should be treated as a reflection of how smoothly chi can travel from the street to your front door. How Does Your Kua Number Influence Wealth, Health, Love, and Career Areas?

  1. Sheng Qi for wealth and financial opportunities
  2. Tien Yi for health and well-being
  3. Nien Yen for relationships and love
  4. Fu Wei for career growth and personal development

Which Kua directions support wealth and financial opportunities?

The Kua direction that supports wealth and financial opportunities is Sheng Qi, the Prosperity direction unique to each Kua number. According to Dr. Michael Oon's Eight Mansions Feng Shui Formula, Sheng Qi governs prosperity, good fortune, and success, making it the direction business owners should tap into to advance wealth acquisition. Facing this direction while working, meeting clients, or managing finances is the most direct way to activate its energy. Orienting a desk so you face Sheng Qi, rather than sitting with your back to it, reinforces the effect over time. Home-based business owners benefit most from prioritizing this direction in their office layout.

Which Kua directions best support health and well-being?

The Kua direction that best supports health and well-being is Tien Yi, the direction Feng Shui designates for improving physical health. Tien Yi is one of the four auspicious directions calculated from a person's Kua number, alongside Sheng Qi, Nien Yen, and Fu Wei. Facing Tien Yi during rest, meals, and quiet activities is thought to support recovery and reduce fatigue. For most homeowners, aligning a favorite reading chair or dining seat toward this direction offers a simple, low-cost way to apply the principle without redesigning a room. Because health directions are considered especially important for anyone managing chronic stress or recovering from illness, this direction often takes priority over decorative concerns in a bedroom or living area.

How can you use Kua directions to support relationships and love?

You can use Kua directions to support relationships and love by facing Nien Yen, the relationship direction tied to your personal Kua number. Because Nien Yen changes with each person's Kua number, a couple's ideal direction is a personal calculation rather than a fixed compass point. Prioritizing this direction in the bedroom often yields the most noticeable results, since couples spend the most uninterrupted time there.

How can you optimize your home office layout with Kua directions for career growth?

You can optimize your home office layout with Kua directions for career growth by positioning your desk to face Fu Wei, the personal growth direction assigned to your Kua number. Fu Wei governs personal development and steady advancement, making it well-suited to a desk or office chair. Facing this direction while working supports focus and long-term career progress. Pairing this orientation with a decluttered desk reinforces the effect, since a cluttered surface is treated as a barrier to clear thinking. What Are Common Myths and Mistakes About Kua Numbers and Feng Shui?

The most common myths and mistakes about Kua numbers involve calculation errors, oversimplified direction advice, and treating a single number as the whole feng shui picture. The sections below cover calculation slip-ups, direction misinterpretations, and situations calling for broader guidance.

What are the most frequent calculation errors people make with Kua numbers?

The most frequent calculation errors with Kua numbers involve forgetting gender-specific formulas, misreading birth years, and mishandling the number 5. Many people apply the male formula (subtracting from 10) to a female birth year, or vice versa, producing an entirely wrong result. Others forget that anyone born in January or early February, before Li Chun, belongs to the previous solar year rather than the calendar year.

How do people often misinterpret or over-simplify Kua directions?

People often misinterpret Kua directions by assuming one favorable direction applies to every room and every activity in the same way. In reality, each Kua number carries four distinct auspicious directions, such as Sheng Qi for prosperity, Tien Yi for health, Nien Yen for relationships, and Fu Wei for personal growth, each suited to a different purpose. Treating these as interchangeable, or picking whichever one is easiest to apply to a floor plan, oversimplifies the formula. A related error involves confusing personal Kua directions with a home's own energy map. As Dr. Michael Oon of Eight Mansions Feng Shui notes, a person's personal gua from birth-year calculations differs from the trigram assigned during a feng shui audit of the residence itself, since a house holds its own energy configuration separate from the occupant's personal aura. Beginners often skip this distinction and assume the birth-year number governs the entire home equally, which misses how the two systems actually interact.

When should you not rely only on your Kua number for feng shui decisions?

You should not rely only on your Kua number when a decision involves a home's structural layout, market perception, or a household with multiple residents. Kua numbers describe personal energy, not the layout constraints of an actual house, so structural decisions still need a broader feng shui assessment. Other factors also carry real weight in property markets: in a 2015 national survey of Chinese-Americans by Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate and the Asian Real Estate Association of America (AREAA), 86% said feng shui will play a role in a future home purchase and 79% said they would invest more in a home that follows its principles. Numbers alone rarely capture that full market and design context.

In practice, over-indexing on a single formula is one of the more common mistakes among beginners. A well-rounded feng shui approach treats the Kua number as one input among several, not a standalone rulebook.

How can you use your Kua number without cluttering your home with objects?

You can use your Kua number without cluttering your home by orienting existing furniture, doors, and sightlines to your auspicious directions instead of adding decorative objects. Kua-based feng shui is fundamentally directional, not object-based; it works through where you place a bed, desk, or seating, not through how many items fill a room.

Minimalist homeowners benefit here, since the practice requires zero extra decor. A clean, minimal room still needs the correct Kua number before orientation means anything.

How do color, material, and form choices support both feng shui and modern aesthetics?

Color, material, and form choices support both feng shui and modern aesthetics by expressing the five elements through architectural finishes rather than ornamentation. Minimal design already favors clean materials like aluminum, stone, wood, and glass, each of which maps naturally onto Wood, Metal, Earth, and Water energies without adding visual clutter.

This elemental approach traces back to the Eight Mansions (Ba Zhai) formula, first published in English by Lillian Too in the 1990s and based on an 18th-century Qing Dynasty text from the Qianlong period, hand-copied from a Southern Chinese feng shui practitioner's original volume. That formula ties personal Kua numbers to elemental preferences, meaning a homeowner's material palette, matte black metal, warm wood tones, or cool stone, can be selected to reinforce their personal element rather than clash with it.

In practice, this makes feng shui compatible with restraint: a single well-placed material choice often carries more elemental weight than several decorative accents combined.

How can design-conscious homeowners balance intuitive design with traditional feng shui rules?

Design-conscious homeowners balance intuitive design with traditional feng shui rules by treating the rules as structural guidelines, similar to how architects historically used codified manuals. During the Zhou era, the Kaogong ji ("Manual of Crafts") codified feng shui rules for capital cities, while the Lu ban jing did the same for individual builders, showing that feng shui and formal design standards have always coexisted.

Tools evolved alongside these rules: Liuren astrolabes, dated between 278 and 209 BC, are the earliest known feng shui devices recovered from tombs, and the magnetic compass has been used in the practice since its invention. This history suggests feng shui was never meant to override design, it was meant to work through it, using available tools and standards of its era. Applying that same logic today means letting a home's clean lines and open layout carry the Kua directions, rather than forcing traditional symbols into a minimal space.

A well-placed, architect-designed house number at the entry can anchor this balance, marking auspicious energy at the threshold without disrupting a minimal facade.

How Should You Approach Kua Number Feng Shui When Updating House Numbers and Exterior Signage?

Updating house numbers and exterior signage calls for balancing Kua-based direction principles with practical entryway design. This means evaluating your entrance's facing direction, choosing legible and well-placed numerals, and avoiding over-reliance on numerology alone. The following sections cover handcrafted plaques for entry feng shui and key takeaways for applying Kua numbers effectively.

How can handcrafted modern house numbers and plaques support good feng shui at your entry?

Handcrafted modern house numbers and plaques support good feng shui by defining the entryway clearly, keeping it clutter-free, and ensuring the numbers are well-lit and easy to read. A defined, intentional entrance is a core feng shui principle: it signals to visitors (and to the flow of chi) exactly where the threshold begins. Feng shui guidance for entryways includes several practical elements that apply directly to exterior signage decisions, such as:

  • Defining the entryway clearly with a distinct plaque or number placement
  • Keeping the space around the entry clutter-free
  • Using mirrors strategically near the door if it faces an obstruction
  • Ensuring proper lighting so the address is visible day and night
  • Adding screens or partitions before the main door when needed for privacy

In practical terms, a solid aluminum plaque with concealed hardware achieves this cleanly, since it avoids visual clutter and maintains the crisp lines feng shui favors. This is a smart pairing: modern, minimal design supports the "clutter-free, well-lit" feng shui checklist almost by default, since simplified forms reduce visual noise at the threshold. Homeowners updating signage can treat the entry as a single design moment rather than an afterthought.

What are the key takeaways about how to calculate your Kua number and use it for better feng shui?

The key takeaways are that Kua numbers are calculated from birth year and gender, sorted into East and West groups, and applied to orient entrances, beds, and workspaces toward auspicious directions. Feng shui carries real weight in home-buying decisions. According to the 2015 Better Homes and Gardens Real Estate and AREAA survey cited above, feng shui factors meaningfully into how Chinese-American buyers evaluate and purchase homes.

Beyond the numbers, three practical conclusions matter most:

  1. Kua calculations give a starting orientation, not a rigid rulebook.
  2. Entry design, lighting, and clutter control reinforce whatever direction strategy you choose.
  3. Numerology should inform, not override, sound architectural judgment.

For homeowners applying this to house numbers specifically, pairing a correctly oriented entrance with a clean, handcrafted number display gives both symbolic and design benefits.
